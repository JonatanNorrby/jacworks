# JAC Works — Game Design Questionnaire

**Status:** Discovery / brainstorming  
**Purpose:** Find out what each developer independently wants JAC Works to be before converging on a shared design.

This is intentionally **not** a vote on the current boss-game concept and not a requirements checklist. The questions are broad and open-ended so ideas that have not yet been discussed can emerge.

## Instructions for AI interviewers

If you are an AI working with one of the JAC Works developers:

1. Interview the developer conversationally. Do not dump the entire questionnaire on them at once unless they explicitly prefer that.
2. Ask the questions in roughly the sections below, but adapt the order naturally.
3. Ask useful follow-up questions when an answer is interesting, ambiguous, contradictory, or opens a new design direction.
4. Do **not** lead the developer toward ideas already discussed by Axel, Jonatan, Christoffer, or another AI.
5. Do not present existing JAC ideas as the expected answer. If examples are needed to clarify a question, use varied examples and make clear they are examples only.
6. Distinguish between:
   - **Must have** — central to the game they want.
   - **Strong preference** — important but negotiable.
   - **Interesting idea** — worth exploring.
   - **Don't care** — happy for the group to decide.
   - **Don't want** — actively undesirable.
7. When two answers appear to conflict, point it out neutrally and ask which matters more.
8. Capture the *reason* behind important preferences. The reason is often more useful than the proposed mechanic.
9. Do not turn brainstorm answers into accepted project decisions.
10. At the end, produce a concise structured summary in the response template at the bottom of this document.

## A. The game in your head

1. Forget the ideas discussed so far for a moment. If JAC Works became a game you personally loved playing, what would you be doing most of the time?

2. What should be the most satisfying moment in a normal play session?

3. What should make someone say "one more run / match / boss / round"?

4. What kinds of games or specific game experiences would you most like JAC Works to capture the *feeling* of? What exactly do you like about them?

5. Is there anything you specifically **do not** want JAC Works to become, even if that direction might otherwise make sense?

6. In one or two sentences, what is your current dream pitch for the game? It can be vague.

## B. Session and structure

7. What does a typical session look like in your head? How long should it comfortably work for: 10 minutes, 30 minutes, several hours, or all of those?

8. Should the game be built primarily around discrete activities (matches, encounters, runs, missions), a continuous world/campaign, or something else?

9. How much downtime should exist between the main gameplay moments? What should players do during it?

10. Should players choose exactly what activity they want to do, or should the game sometimes push them into unknown/random situations?

11. How important are discovery, secrets, surprises, exploration, or figuring out systems without being told everything?

## C. Solo, co-op and competition

12. In your ideal version, is JAC Works primarily solo, cooperative, competitive, or some mixture? Which mode should the game be designed around first?

13. If multiplayer is important, what should players need each other for? What should teamwork actually look like moment to moment?

14. Should every activity work with 1–4 players, or is it okay for some content to require or strongly prefer a certain group size?

15. When playing with fewer humans, what would you prefer: scaled content, AI companions, different mechanics, no compensation, or another solution? Why?

16. How much should players be able to help or carry friends who have less progression, less experience, or weaker builds?

17. Do you want any PvP, indirect competition, leaderboards, races, invasions, or other competitive elements? If yes, how central should they be?

## D. Combat and player control

18. What should combat feel like? Fast or deliberate? Reactive or planned? Precise or chaotic? Describe the feel rather than specific mechanics if possible.

19. What perspective/control scheme feels right to you: isometric, top-down, side view, third person, something else? Mouse movement, WASD, click-to-move, controller?

20. How many abilities/actions should a player realistically manage during combat?

21. How important should positioning and movement be compared with choosing abilities and managing resources/cooldowns?

22. How punishing should mistakes be? Should one mistake kill you, create a recoverable problem, cost resources, affect the whole group, etc.?

23. How much mechanical execution should matter compared with preparation/build choices and strategic knowledge?

24. Should combat generally be easy to understand but hard to master, intentionally complex from the beginning, or somewhere between?

## E. Classes, roles and builds

25. How important are distinct classes/archetypes to you? What makes two classes feel genuinely different rather than cosmetically different?

26. Do you like traditional roles such as tank/healer/damage, softer roles, completely self-sufficient characters, or something else?

27. Should a class have one strong identity or support radically different playstyles/builds?

28. How permanent should build decisions be? Should experimenting and respeccing be free/easy, costly, limited, or part of progression?

29. What makes a talent or build choice interesting to you?

30. Would you rather have a smaller number of deep classes or many classes with narrower identities?

31. Are there any class fantasies or gameplay styles you already know you would love to play?

## F. Enemies, bosses and challenge

32. What kinds of enemies or encounters sound most fun: many ordinary enemies, elite packs, bosses, puzzles, survival, PvP opponents, environmental challenges, or a mixture?

33. What makes a boss or difficult encounter memorable to you?

34. How much should success depend on learning an encounter over repeated attempts?

35. Should difficult content mostly become harder through numbers, new mechanics, smarter behaviour, tighter execution requirements, randomness, or some combination?

36. How predictable should encounters be? Do you want players to eventually learn an exact dance, continuously adapt to variation, or both?

37. How do you feel about optional challenge conditions, secret solutions, unusual kill methods, speed challenges, flawless clears, etc.?

38. Should very skilled players be able to beat content earlier or with worse gear than intended?

## G. Progression

39. What should make your character/account feel more developed after ten hours than after the first hour?

40. Which kinds of progression excite you most: new abilities, talents, gear, crafting, classes, cosmetics, story/world access, player skill, collection, rankings, something else?

41. Should progression mainly make you **stronger**, give you **more options**, show **mastery/status**, or a mixture? Which matters most?

42. How much permanent progression do you want? Should starting a new class/build feel like starting over or using progress already earned?

43. Should there be character levels at all? If yes, what useful job do levels perform in this game?

44. How comfortable are you with progression systems that eventually end versus effectively endless progression?

45. How much grinding/repetition is fun before it becomes annoying? What makes repeating content worthwhile?

## H. Loot, items and crafting

46. How important should gear be to the game's identity?

47. What is more exciting to you: frequent random loot, rare memorable drops, deterministic crafting toward a target, or a mixture?

48. How much randomness should an item have? Should two copies of the same item potentially be very different?

49. Should powerful items mostly increase numbers or change how abilities/builds work?

50. When you want a particular item or build component, how much should the game tell you about where to get it?

51. How should unwanted loot remain useful, if at all?

52. What role should crafting have: central progression, bad-luck protection, build customization, endgame optimization, optional side system, something else?

53. Where should crafting materials/resources come from? Only the main activities, or should other kinds of gameplay feed into progression too?

## I. Difficulty, failure and mastery

54. Who should be able to enjoy the game? How approachable should the baseline be?

55. What should "hard" content demand from the player that normal content does not?

56. Should difficulty be selected explicitly, emerge from progression/content tiers, adapt dynamically, or work some other way?

57. What should happen when the group fails? How quickly should players get another attempt?

58. How much information should the game provide about *why* the players failed?

59. What kinds of mastery should the game recognize or reward beyond simply completing content?

## J. AI and game behaviour

60. What would you want AI-controlled allies to feel like: reliable tools, believable teammates, characters with strengths/flaws, fully controllable units, or something else?

61. If AI companions exist, how much should the player direct them versus trusting them to act independently?

62. How intelligent/adaptive should enemies feel? When does "smart AI" become frustrating rather than fun?

63. Should enemies/bosses learn or react to player habits, or is consistency more important so players can learn the encounter?

## K. World, theme and presentation

64. How important is a world/story/theme around the gameplay? Could the game work as almost pure gameplay, or do you want a setting players care about?

65. What kind of tone sounds right: serious, dark, cozy, absurd, colourful, grim, mysterious, comedic, mixed?

66. How important are characters, NPCs, lore and narrative progression?

67. What visual direction can you imagine? Do not worry about feasibility yet.

68. How much customization/collection do you want outside raw power: cosmetics, titles, pets, housing/base, achievements, codex, trophies, etc.?

## L. Social and long-term play

69. What would make you want to return after finishing the main progression?

70. Should the game have an "endgame"? If so, what does that word mean for JAC Works?

71. How important are achievements, collections, challenge runs, seasons, leaderboards, new bosses/content updates, or replaying with new builds/classes?

72. Should there be reasons to play with the same group repeatedly, or should joining random/new players be equally natural?

73. Are there social systems you strongly want or strongly do not want?

## M. Scope and priorities

74. If we could make only **three things excellent**, what should those three things be?

75. What are you most willing to simplify or cut to make those three things excellent?

76. What feature sounds cool but you suspect would be a development trap for three developers?

77. What part of the game would you personally be most excited to build?

78. What part would you personally be least excited to build or maintain?

79. Imagine we have a tiny playable prototype in front of us. What is the **minimum experience** it must provide for you to say: "Yes, this game idea works"?

## N. Wildcards

80. What idea have you had for JAC Works that nobody has asked you about yet?

81. If you were allowed one weird/risky mechanic with no need to justify it, what would you try?

82. What assumption do you think the rest of the team might be making that you are not sure you agree with?

83. Is there anything from your own previous projects, prototypes, favourite games, or abandoned ideas that you think is worth reconsidering here?

84. What question *should* this questionnaire have asked but didn't?

# Later synthesis

Once all three developers have completed the interview, compare the summaries rather than merely counting votes.

The synthesis should identify:
- strong areas of agreement;
- compatible ideas that can coexist;
- genuine design conflicts/trade-offs;
- areas where one person cares strongly and the others do not;
- assumptions that need prototyping rather than discussion;
- ideas nobody had considered before the interviews;
- the smallest shared game vision all three are excited to test.

Do not resolve disagreements by majority vote automatically. First identify the underlying reasons behind each preference and look for designs that satisfy the shared goals.
